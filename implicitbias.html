What is Implicit Bias?

Implicit bias refers to the attitudes or stereotypes that affect our understanding, actions, and decisions without us being fully aware of them. These biases are often developed through social experiences, media exposure, culture, and the environments we grow up in. Implicit bias does not necessarily mean a person is intentionally prejudiced. Instead, it highlights how people can make quick associations and assumptions automatically.

Implicit biases can affect many areas of life, including education, hiring, healthcare, criminal justice, and technology. Understanding these biases is an important step toward recognizing how they influence everyday decisions and how we can work toward more equitable outcomes.

How Implicit Bias Can Influence Decisions

  • Social Messages: Media, culture, and personal experiences
  • Automatic Associations: Unconscious assumptions and stereotypes
  • Snap Judgments: Quick perceptions and decisions
  • Possible Outcomes: Effects on hiring, education, and technology

Implicit bias can influence decisions without us realizing it. Recognizing these patterns can help us question our assumptions.

What is Intersectionality?

Intersectionality is a framework that helps explain how different aspects of a person's identity combine to shape their experiences. Factors such as race, gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, disability, and sexuality do not exist independently from one another. Instead, they interact and often create unique challenges or advantages.

For example, the experiences of a woman in technology may differ from those of a man in technology. Similarly, a woman of color may face different challenges than a white woman due to the intersections of both race and gender. Understanding intersectionality helps us move beyond one-dimensional views of identity and recognize the complexity of people's lived experiences.

The Implicit Bias Test (IBT)

The Implicit Bias Test (often referred to as the Implicit Association Test or IAT) measures how quickly people associate certain groups with specific concepts. Participants sort words and images into categories, and reaction times are used to identify patterns of automatic associations.

The purpose of the test is not to label individuals as biased or unbiased. Instead, it encourages self-reflection by revealing associations that may exist beneath conscious awareness. Many people are surprised by their results because they often differ from their personal beliefs and values.

Our Reflection on the IBT

One of the most valuable aspects of taking the Implicit Bias Test was not necessarily the final result, but the opportunity to reflect on how biases are formed. During our discussion, we observed that the test challenged us to think about assumptions we may make automatically.

Jesse found it interesting that they consistently associated men with science and careers more quickly than women, even after the categories were switched. Another noted that the test was mentally challenging and forced them to think more carefully about how the brain creates associations.

Personally, I (Joel) found that the most important takeaway was understanding how assumptions can develop without conscious intention. The test demonstrated that many biases operate automatically and are influenced by society, culture, media, and our personal experiences. Even when we strongly believe in fairness and equality, self-awareness remains important. Reflecting on our assumptions allows us to better understand ourselves and how we interact with different groups of people.

Within technology, this reflection is especially important. Software, algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, and digital platforms are all created by people. If bias exists in data or design decisions, it can unintentionally affect users. Recognizing implicit bias is therefore an important step toward creating more inclusive technologies.
